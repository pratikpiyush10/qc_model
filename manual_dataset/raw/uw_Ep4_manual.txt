① Theme & Narrative Core
Core Theme: A title can be taken away by decree, but duty cannot. The prince the court calls a bad omen becomes the only one who guards the gate, and his power wakes when he chooses to protect rather than flee.

Emotional Spine:

Tier 1 (Blamed Again): On the temple steps under the spreading crack, Someshwar blames the dark threat on Agnivesh, silences Devika's defence and sends him to the Archive Tower under guard. Badrinath watches; Ranvijay stays behind with the Ancestral Sword.
Tier 2 (The Truth and the Choice): On the tower stairs the silent warning conches raise the first question. Devika's map and compass reveal that the northern gate was disabled from inside the Surya Mandir. She begs him to flee with the map; he refuses: the court exiled him, the people did not.
Tier 3 (The Gatekeeper): The Shadow Hounds overrun the harbour barracks. The guards refuse his command; he takes a spear and runs alone. He saves a sentry with one thrown spear, sends the wounded inside, holds the gate alone, and when three hounds strike at once time stops, the sword-shaped mark blazes and golden fire burns two to ash. The pack retreats, and on a black rock at the water's edge a woman's silhouette with violet eyes turns away.
Exit Takeaway & Expression Strategy: The audience's sympathy for an outcast turns into awe as he finally fights back. The cliffhanger is the reveal of the hidden enemy, not the victory: the hard cut lands on Kaalini turning into the mist.

Natural Acting Direction (all scenes)
Grounded and real: Every performance is understated and believable, played as if no camera were there. No theatrical poses, no stage gestures, no mugging to the lens.
Eyes and micro-expressions over big gestures:
Emotion reads through the eyes, breath, the jaw and small shifts of weight.
Examples: Agnivesh looking down at the silent conches (12), Devika's fingertip on the violet line (15), her eyes welling as she blocks the stairs (37–38), Guard 1 lowering his head (34).
Dialogue:
Spoken at a natural pace, verbatim in Hindi (Devanagari in every prompt), never declaimed. Every dialogue shot stays at or under 2.8 words per second.
Pauses come from thought, not from the camera.
Where intensity rises:
Only for the antagonists and for extreme emotion: Someshwar's fury, Devika's pleas, the sentries' terror, Agnivesh's final warning.
Even then it stays believable. Someshwar is a cold king, not a ranting tyrant. Badrinath says nothing and only watches, hands folded. The guards' refusal is stiff and cold, not sneering.
Stillness as power: Agnivesh never struggles against the guards and never raises his voice on the stairs. His first loud line is the command at the gate (61). The fight is economical: a throw, a thrust, a raised wrist. The speed comes from the camera and the speed ramps, not from flourishes.
Listening: Every character is alive when not speaking. Guard 2 stays silent and does not move when Agnivesh takes Guard 1's spear (36). The wounded sentries are hurt and frightened, not heroic.
② Visual Style & Framing Tone (Native 9:16 Vertical)
Vertical Composition Rules:

Native 9:16: Every shot is composed for 9:16 vertical, with all characters fully inside frame. Every shot line in the prompts carries a "Sides:" note saying what sits in the top, middle and bottom thirds.
Two side by side at most: Groups of three or more are stacked in depth, never in a horizontal line. On the steps the court is layered by height: Someshwar, Badrinath and Ranvijay at the top; Devika on the steps between; Agnivesh and the guards lower. At the gate the three sentries and the hounds are layered from the gate to the beach.
Centre third: Faces, eyelines and key hands (the compass on the map, the fingertip on the line, the spear, the blazing wrist) stay in the centre vertical third.
Using height:
The sky and the steps: the camera rides the crack across the shell (1), pulls back over the whole citadel (2), cranes up the temple steps (3), and lifts up and back over Ranvijay as the procession leaves (9).
The tower: a crane up the stairwell (11); the arch on the top landing with the harbour and the Rajmahal beyond (14, 16–24, 33); the high angle down the open well toward the conches (37, 40).
The POV: from the arch down onto the gate and the shore (24, 32).
The gate: ground-level angles on the stalking and biting hounds (42, 44, 46, 54); low angles on the leaps and the wall-top landing (50, 62); the reverse out to the sea for the charge, the bullet-time orbit and the fire (69–75).
The dome: the cracked Raksha-Kavach is the ceiling over Sc 1, seen through the arch in Sc 2/4, and a faint shimmer over the shore in Sc 3/5.
Composition Style & Narrative Rationale:

Up vs Down: Sc 1 shoots the king from below and Agnivesh from above while he is condemned. In the tower they sit together at the same level. At the gate the camera drops low on Agnivesh once he chooses to fight (65, 69, 75).
Screen Line (continues Ep 3): Someshwar, Badrinath and Ranvijay are top and screen-left on the steps; Agnivesh and the guards are lower, screen-right; Devika is between them. In the tower Devika sits screen-left and Agnivesh screen-right, with the arch upper-right. At the gate the base view looks from the beach toward the gate; the reverse (Reverse From the Gate Toward the Shore) looks out to the sea and the black rock.
The reverses: The tower derivatives and the shore reverse are true reverses, so screen sides flip in those shots only (24, 32, 37, 40, 54–57, 69–75, 77, 79). Track start and end states stay in the forward orientation.
Three-Layer Spatial Separation:

Foreground: The guards' gripping hands and spear shafts, the conches, the map and compass, the near gate post, the impaled hound.
Midground: The principals.
Background: The court and the cracked dome (Sc 1); the arch, harbour and Rajmahal (Sc 2/4); the gate, wall torches and tower on the cliff, or the beach, sea and black rock (Sc 3/5).
Cinematography Direction
Camera Movement:

Flowing moves for the drama: Slow push-ins, track-ins and track-backs, arcs, orbits, cranes, tilts and rack focus, each motivated by a move, a look or a reveal. No slow sideways pans.
FAST beats, Marvel-style (24 shots: 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 36, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 50, 51, 52, 53, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74, 75): the action is cut on short 2–5s shots with lateral tracks, crash-ins, snap zooms, speed ramps and bullet time, exactly as written in each shot. The key ones:
The hounds hit the sentries, seen in a 2s-a-shot run (26–32).
Agnivesh snatches Guard 1's spear and turns down the stairs (34, quick follow).
The barracks overrun: the stalk, the swipe, the bite and the drag (41–45).
The leap and the throw: a fast tilt with the leap (50), then a speed ramp from a fast track into slow motion as the spear flies past the sentry into the hound (52), and a snap back to real time (53).
The climax (69–75): the charge, the fast lateral track with the two leaps, a 180-degree bullet-time orbit while the hounds hang in the air (71), the impact, a speed ramp as the spear butt hits the ground (73), a crash zoom onto the wrist (74), and extreme slow motion as the golden fire leaves the mark, then a hard ramp to full speed (75).
Static holds (8, for emphasis: 12, 46, 48, 54, 56, 57, 58, 66): the silent conches with a rack focus to Agnivesh (12); the dropped sword at ground level (46); the hound landing dead at the sentry's feet (54); the pack stopping to stare (56); Devika frozen in shock (58).
No jump cuts. No two consecutive shots share the same size and angle.
Shot-Size Variety:

Extreme wides: The citadel under the cracked dome (2), the POV onto the shore (24).
Wides: The steps (3), the procession (9), the stairwell (11), the horn (22), the POV of the overrun (32), the gate (41, 49, 67), the dead hound at ground level (54), the charge (69) and the retreat (77).
Mediums and medium wides: Carry the guards, the sitting exchange and the fight.
Medium close-ups and close-ups: For the verdict, the reading of the map, the refusals and the sentries' cries.
Inserts and extreme close-ups: The map and compass top-down (15), the conches (12), the dropped sword (46), the smoke from the dead hound's mouth (55), the blazing wrist (74), Kaalini's violet eyes opening the last dolly out (79).
Over-the-shoulder shots (35, 37) keep the two people in an exchange in the same frame.
Lens Choices & Depth of Field: full-frame anamorphic; 24–32mm wides with deep focus, 40–50mm mediums, 75–85mm close-ups with shallow focus; 100mm macro for the inserts (15, 46, 55, 74); flare only from real light.

Rack focus: from the conches to Agnivesh (12).
Look: Photorealistic live-action, natural skin texture, real fabric, metal and fur. No CGI look, no plastic skin.

Emotional Curve:

It opens on public condemnation under a breaking sky (Sc 1).
It drops into a quiet, intimate discovery and a choice on the stairs (Sc 2).
It snaps into fast action with the attack, then into the guards' cold refusal (Sc 3–4).
It builds through the fight at the gate to the bullet-time release of the mark, and ends cold on the hidden enemy (Sc 5).

④ Scene-by-Scene Emotion & Camera Intent
Scene 1: EXT. SURYAGARH CITADEL - NIGHT (CONTINUOUS) (Shots 1–10 / 48s)
Emotional Target: The court's fear turned into blame; a public verdict without a trial.
Atmosphere Direction: Ominous, windswept, the sky breaking overhead.
Camera Intent:
The sky (1–2): a follow shot along the leading edge of the crack (Raksha-Kavach Cracked (Violet Thunder)), first frame matching Ep 3's last frames; a slow pull back over the citadel (Suryagarh Citadel).
The court (3): a slow crane up the steps from the lower steps (Temple Steps, Low Angle).
The plant (4): a slow push-in on Badrinath, hands folded, no staff.
The accusation (5): a slow arc as Someshwar turns to the guards and points.
The grip (6): a slow push-in from the king's eyeline down onto Agnivesh, the city behind.
The plea and the reply (7–8): low push-in on Devika; a slow dolly back as Someshwar takes one step down toward her.
The exit (9): a slow crane up and back over Ranvijay's shoulder as the procession crosses the citadel toward the tower.
Spatial Staging & Distance: Someshwar at the top, Badrinath at his shoulder, Ranvijay beside them (screen-left, top); Agnivesh held a few steps below (Guard 1 on his left arm, Guard 2 on his right), screen-right; Devika on the steps between the two groups; Royal Courtiers in depth by the doors. The guards' swords are sheathed and they carry no spears yet.
Acting Notes:
Someshwar: Cold fury, sure he is protecting the kingdom. One step down at "Bas, Devika!", no more.
Devika: Firm, not hysterical; she names the traitor inside.
Agnivesh: Looks at the guards' hands, does not resist, says nothing.
Badrinath: Silent, hands folded.
Ranvijay: Grips the Ancestral Sword's hilt as the procession leaves; not drawn.
Lighting Plan: Warm fire-bowl key, cool moonlight, the golden dome glow and the violet crack flickering as a rim; violet thunder flashes on 1.

Lighting Plan: Warm torchlight on the stairs, cool moonlight through the arch as rim and fill; the violet line and the gold gate symbols on the map light her fingertip (15–16).

Emotional Target: Abandonment by his own soldiers, and the decision to go alone.
Atmosphere Direction: Tense, cold; the horn still echoing.
Camera Intent:
The command (33): a circular track around Agnivesh as he turns from the arch to the guards.
The refusal (34–35): a low push-in on Guard 1's lowered head, then over Agnivesh's shoulder.
The spear (36): a quick follow as he snatches Guard 1's spear and turns down (FAST 8).

Scene 5: EXT. HARBOR BARRACKS GATE - NIGHT (CONTINUOUS) (Shots 41–79 / 133s)
Emotional Target: Desperation turning into awe, then dread.
Atmosphere Direction: Brutal, close, wet stone and surf; then the eerie stillness of the retreat.
Camera Intent:
The overrun (41–48): FAST 9–13 (a push toward the gate, a low track with the stalking hound, a snap tilt with the swipe, a snap zoom on the bite, a handheld lateral track with the drag), then a locked-off insert of the dropped sword and a slow crane down on the pinned sentry.

The stillness (54–59): locked-off at ground level on the reverse as the hound lands dead (Reverse From the Gate Toward the Shore); a slow drift over the smoke from its mouth; locked-off on the staring pack, on Devika, and on the sentry's trembling face.
The cries and the command (60–67): a track-in on the sentry; a track back with Agnivesh and the wounded; a tilt up to the hound on the wall and a drift down the splitting stone; a crane up as he pulls a spear from the rack; a crane down over the hounds' backs as he paces across the gateway.
The climax (68–75): the warning cut off; FAST 18–24 as described above.
The aftermath (76–79): a push-in past Devika and the sentries; a push-in past the impaled hound as the pack retreats; a slow dolly out from Kaalini's eyes to her silhouette.
Spatial Staging & Distance: The base view looks from the beach toward the gate; the reverse looks out from the gate apron to the beach, the sea and the black rock. Agnivesh's back is to the gate from 67 on; the wounded, Devika and the sentries are inside the gateway behind him.
Acting Notes: The Royal Sentry terrified and hurt, his anger at Ranvijay is panic, not politics; Agnivesh loud only for the command (61) and the last warning (76), cold and clear for the cut-off warning (68); Devika frozen at the throw (58), then practical with the wounded; Kaalini a silhouette only, no face detail, unhurried.
Lighting Plan: Cool moonlight on the water and sand, warm wall torches on the stone, sea mist, the cracked dome with violet thunder flicker overhead; violet hound eyes and purple hound smoke; at 75 the golden fire floods the shore, and a warm gold glow stays on his wrist through 77.
Lighting Direction (all scenes)
Match the plate: Every shot is lit to match its location asset: the same direction, colour temperature, contrast and haze.
Period-correct sources only: Torches, fire bowls and moonlight. The map symbols, the dome, the crack, the hound eyes and the mark are motivated gold or violet light sources. No electric light.
Contrast: Low-key chiaroscuro throughout. Highlights stay on faces and hands in the centre third.
Time: The whole episode plays at night; every track prompt opens with "at night".
Smoke Rules (Nin's style, applied to every prompt)
The Shadow Hounds trail violet-purple smoke as they move, leak it from any wound, and their smoke stays purple when they die. Never black or grey on the hounds.
v5: the storyboard itself is now purple everywhere (55, 60, 73). Hound #1 dissolves into purple smoke in its own shot (57, 3s); the impaled hound stays on the planted spear to the end of Ep 4, leaking purple (73, 77, 78), and dissolves in Ep 5 shots 4–5. This deviates from the script's "black smoke" by Nin's approval.
The sea mist around Kaalini is tinged purple (79).
Demon smoke is purple (or purple-and-black). Non-magical props give black smoke only (none in this episode).
